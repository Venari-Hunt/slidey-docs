Writing slides

A slide note is an ordinary note with one property that tells Slidey how to split it.

Pick a way with the slides: property at the top of the note. The easiest way to set it is to type /slides in the note (see Slide menu).

slides:What becomes a slideGood for
headingsEvery heading starts a slideNotes you already write with headings
separators (or no slides:)Each part between --- linesClassic Markdown slide decks
blocksOnly the parts you wrap in markersA long note where only a few parts go on screen

Headings mode

---
slides: headings
---
# Title of the talk

## First point
Text for the first point.

## Second point
- A bullet
- Another bullet
  • Every heading (# to ######) starts a new slide. Any text before the first heading gets its own slide.
  • The heading level can pick a layout and a style automatically: Settings → Heading levels. By default a # heading uses the title layout.
  • A --- line is just a horizontal line here, not a new slide.
  • To leave a section out of the deck, put %% noslide %% right under its heading.

Separators mode

---
slides: separators
---
# First slide

---

# Second slide

--

A slide below the second one (a vertical slide).
  • --- on its own line starts the next slide.
  • -- starts a slide below the current one. You reach it with ↓. Use it for optional detail.
  • A note with no slides: property works this way too.

Blocks mode

---
slides: blocks
---
Research notes, links, ideas… none of this is on a slide.

%% slide %%
# The one slide I'll show
%% endslide %%

More private notes.

%% slide layout=quote %%
> A quote for the second slide.
%% endslide %%
  • %% slide %% starts a slide. %% endslide %% ends it. The next %% slide %% or the end of the note also ends it.
  • Everything outside the markers stays out of the deck.
  • You can pick a layout, style or background on the marker line itself: %% slide layout=quote style=paper %%.

Slide markers

Lines like %% layout=two-column %% are slide markers. Obsidian hides %% … %% text in reading view, so markers never clutter your note. Put them at the top of a slide: right under the heading, right after the ---, or on the %% slide %% line.

MarkerWhat it does
%% layout=two-column %%Where things go on the slide. See layouts.
%% style=night %%Colors and fonts. See styles.
%% bg=photo.jpg %%A background picture or color. See backgrounds.
%% notes %%Everything after it on this slide is speaker notes, not shown on the slide.
%% color=#eee accent=red size=1.4 %%One-off changes for this slide only. See one-off changes.
%% noslide %%Headings mode: leave this section out.

You can combine several on one line: %% layout=image-left style=paper bg=#223344 %%.

Dots and labels in the editor

In a slide note, each slide's first line gets a colored dot in the margin and a small label at the end, like two-column · night. They show which layout and style the slide will use, before you look at the preview.

  • A misspelled name shows in red with a question mark: nigth?
  • plain means no layout or style was picked.
  • not a slide (struck through) marks a %% noslide %% section.

A plain --- note gets the dots once its properties include slides, layout, style, preset or theme.

Settings for the whole deck

These properties at the top of the note apply to every slide:

---
slides: headings
layout: title
style: paper
theme: black
---

A marker on one slide always wins over the deck setting.
